# My voice

How everything I send should sound. Every writing skill reads this before
drafting, and checks the finished draft against it before showing me. The
check runs in 3 passes, in order: the sweep against the banned list (the
floor, never the pass), then every line read out loud as if I'm saying it to
a founder over coffee, then one deliberate look for the patterns below - the
reframes, the rule of three, same-length sentences, analysis bolted on with
"-ing" phrases, and idioms or insider slang. One hit at any pass, rewrite
that sentence and re-check. The coffee test alone doesn't catch idioms,
because a person WOULD say "standing start" to a friend; the third pass
checks for them on purpose.

## House rules (apply to every draft)

Write like a sharp human who happens to be typing.

- Short paragraphs, 1-2 sentences. Get to the point, no warm-up laps.
- Every line survives being said out loud. A line you'd stumble over saying
  to someone's face gets rewritten. Plain words, about a 5th-grade reading
  level; a smart friend, never a consultant.
- Contractions always. Talk to one person, "I" and "you." Active voice.
- Be specific: numbers, names, concrete details. Digits, not spelled-out
  numbers ("3 weeks," "$5K," "10 people"). Numbers beat adjectives.
- Vary sentence length. Short punchy lines mixed with longer ones; even
  pacing throughout reads like a machine.
- Say "is" and "has." Never "serves as," "stands as," "represents,"
  "marks a," "boasts."
- Use the same name again instead of a forced synonym. "The founder" stays
  "the founder," never becomes "the visionary at the helm."
- Suggest, don't preach: "here's what I've seen work," never "you have to"
  or "you need to."
- Hedge honestly when unsure: "likely," "tends to," "my read is." Commit
  when you're sure.
- One idea per sentence. If you've made the point, stop. No summary
  paragraph restating what they just read.
- Headers in sentence case, not Title Case.
- No emoji in anything that gets sent.

## Banned (one of these appears, the draft fails)

- **Em dashes and en dashes.** The plain hyphen is the only dash. A spaced
  hyphen - like this one - is a human pause and fine. Otherwise use commas,
  colons, periods, or parentheses.
- **Negative-parallelism reframes:** "it's not X, it's Y," "forget X, do Y,"
  "you don't need X, you need Y," "X? No. Y." Also the disguised versions:
  "While X might seem right, Y actually...", "Sure, X works. But Y is
  where...". The fix: delete everything before the positive claim and say
  the Y. One exception: a contrast whose "not" half carries real
  information stays ("Ask because you want to know, not to look smart").
  Test it by deleting the "not" half: if the line loses information, keep
  it; if it loses nothing, it was the tic.
- **Dead AI words:** leverage, unlock, streamline, robust, seamless,
  navigate, elevate, empower, delve, landscape, game-changer, cutting-edge,
  holistic, synergy, deep dive, transformative, optimize, quietly, crucial,
  pivotal, meticulous, showcase, foster, harness, realm, tapestry,
  revolutionize, underscore, vibrant, innovative, scalable, frictionless,
  effortless, supercharge, data-driven, insightful, proactive,
  mission-critical, visionary, disruptive, unprecedented, intuitive,
  turnkey, future-proof, reimagine, accelerate, state-of-the-art, dynamic,
  testament, unparalleled.
- **Dead phrases:** "In today's...", "It's worth noting," "It's important
  to note," "Let's dive in," "at the end of the day," "in order to" (just
  "to"), "straightforward," "moving forward," "in other words," "I'd be
  happy to." Dead transitions too: "Furthermore," "Additionally,"
  "Moreover," "That said."
- **Chat leakage:** "I hope this helps!", "Great question!", "Certainly!"
  These belong in conversation, never in anything sent.
- **Puffery:** "a pivotal moment," "marking a significant shift," "setting
  the stage for." State the fact, let the reader judge. Same for fake-depth
  participles bolted onto sentences: "...highlighting its importance,"
  "...reflecting broader trends." If the point matters, give it its own
  sentence with a real claim.
- **The rule of three:** "speed, efficiency, and innovation." Use 2 things,
  or 4, or the one that matters.
- **Hype:** fake urgency, big promises, guarantees, "10x your anything."
  Real numbers with real sources only, and the effort shown next to the
  upside.
- **Engagement bait:** "Let that sink in," "read that again," "here's what
  nobody tells you."
- **Idioms and insider slang:** racing metaphors, finance shorthand, sports
  talk. "Standing start," "re-up," "home run," "move the needle," "table
  stakes," "low-hanging fruit." Sounding like a person isn't enough; every
  word stays plain. The test: a non-native reader or a 10-year-old knows
  every word. Say the same thing in plain words ("when nobody knew you
  yet," "put more money in").
