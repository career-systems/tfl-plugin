# Value-add template

The skeleton tfl-write-value-add fills. One format, five sections, always in this
order. The draft runs about **300 to 500 words across up to two pages** - short
enough that a founder reads it in a minute or two. When the member's offer runs
longer than 6 weeks, the sprint grows to 2 or 3 phases (see the note at the end);
nothing else changes. The section order never changes.

**Bracket sources (2026-08-13).** [Function] in the title and [function] in the
close are the same word: the function word from the member's title (in
`about/tfl-fractional-offer.md` on plugin runs; the approved offer card on
playbook builds). Titles carry no slashes, so the word is unambiguous, and the
title line always matches the Snapshot name the member's messages reference.
[Name] and [LinkedIn URL] are the member's own: the URL comes from the resume
header in their playbook doc, and when the resume doesn't carry it, ask for it -
never ship a placeholder. The wedge line's [length] reads adjectivally, the way
a person types it ("a 2 week Delivery Audit," never "a 2 weeks Delivery Audit");
the numbers come from the offer unchanged. And when the sprint's [N] equals 2,
the fix line reads "Week 2:", never "Weeks 2-2" (both approved 2026-08-13).

```
# [Company] - [Function] Snapshot

From [Name]([LinkedIn URL]), Fractional [title]. I built this page based on
what I can see from the outside, common bottlenecks at your size, and how I
generally remove bottlenecks.

### What I can see from the outside
- 3 plain bullets, 40 words max each, no bold lead-ins. Each one is a
  checkable fact from the confirmed company profile, then what it likely means
  for THIS company, in words the member would say out loud. Neutral or
  positive only. The "what it likely means" half credits the founder's work or
  names the scope they built; it never names a pain. Pain belongs in the
  Bottlenecks section, and an observation written as a bottleneck makes the
  page say everything twice, which is usually why a draft runs over the word
  budget. The second half is optional: when the fact lands on its own, stop at
  the fact, because a "what it means" line added to satisfy the shape turns
  into a generalisation that sounds knowing and says nothing checkable.
  Stopping at the fact is allowed only when the bare fact still does a job
  alone: it credits work or shows scope the founder would nod at. A fact
  that reads as trivia on its own means the fix is a different fact, never
  a shorter bullet. Sentiment test: the founder nods "yep" at all 3.

### Typical bottlenecks at your size
(These are patterns I see at similar companies)

- Up to 3 bullets, 1 or 2 short sentences each. Copied word for word from the company
  profile's Bottlenecks section. Stage-level phrasing only ("tends to," "usually,"
  "often"); never worded as a verdict about them.

### How I generally remove bottlenecks
(For a company like [Company], I usually start with a [length] [Wedge Name])

**Week 1:** Understand. One line: the audit.
**Weeks 2-[N]:** Fix. One or two lines: fix or build one thing. [N] and the
wedge line's name and length come from the member's offer in
about/tfl-fractional-offer.md.

We fill the scores in during the first weeks with your real numbers.

| Goal | Good when… | Today's Score |
| **[Metric 1]** | [plain good condition] | TBD |

### If it's helpful
Exactly these 2 sentences, brackets filled in: "If any of this is even
directionally right, I'm happy to run it with your actual situation and see if
Fractional [title] work could make sense for [Company].

Worst case, you walk away with a clearer [function] scorecard you can run internally or hand to your next full-time hire."

### Quick background
I'm [Name](LinkedIn URL). I've been following [Company]'s growth while doing
fractional work. Recently, I've:
- 3 bullets, each a career win with a real number, picked because they fit this
  company and offer. Tell the win about the kind of business, never a big brand
  name ("Grown a mid-market retail media business by 800%+," not "Grown Walmart
  Connect's mid-market segment by 800%+"): a founder at the member's target size
  should feel understood, never dwarfed.
```

Caps recap: 1 fixed from-line, 3 observations, up to 3 bottlenecks, a 1-line
wedge intro, a 2-line sprint, 4 or 5 scorecard rows (4 real rows beat 5 with a padded one), a
2-sentence close, 3 background bullets, 300 to 500 words total. The fixed lines (the from-line, the wedge line, and the scorecard line) never change
per company; only the brackets get filled. If a section wants to run longer, the
asset is doing too much. Hold the line. Come down by cutting whole things: a
bullet, a clause, a scorecard row (4 real rows beat 5 padded ones), never by
shortening a sentence past the point where it still reads out loud. A broken
sentence costs more than 60 extra words.

## When the offer runs longer than 6 weeks

A 2-line sprint undersells a long engagement: the founder needs to see the phases to
believe the path. When the sprint length in `about/tfl-fractional-offer.md` runs
longer than 6 weeks (a 90-day setup, a quarterly build), the sprint becomes 2 or 3
phases, one line each, with week ranges that sum to the offer length. The shape:
early weeks map and pick the highest-value targets, the middle builds the first
fixes, the final stretch tunes on real use and hands over a playbook or roadmap the
founder keeps either way.

Everything else holds: same five sections, same order, same 300-to-500-word budget,
same close, 4 or 5 scorecard rows, 3 background bullets. Wordiness kills replies faster
than missing detail does.
